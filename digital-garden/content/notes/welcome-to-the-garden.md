This isn’t a blog. Blogs are streams: you post something, it slides down the page, and after a week nobody sees it again. A garden is different. Things get planted here when they’re still rough, and I come back to tend them over time.[^stream]

The idea is older than it sounds. Mark Bernstein wrote about *hypertext gardens* back in {{1998}}, and a small wave of people started building them again around {{2019}}.[^history]

## How to read this place

Every note is marked with how grown it is:

- **Seedlings** are early ideas. Rough, unedited, possibly wrong.
- **Budding** notes have been cleaned up and are taking shape.
- **Evergreen** notes are as finished as anything here gets.

Notes link to each other a lot. Hover over a link to get a quick preview before you click. If a link looks faded and dashed, that note [[Things I want to write about|hasn’t been planted yet]].

A good place to start is [[A note on sidenotes and dates]], which shows off the little features that make this garden work.

[^stream]: Mike Caulfield calls this the difference between *the stream* and *the garden*. The stream is about time, and the garden is about how ideas connect.
[^history]: Maggie Appleton’s [history of digital gardens](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history) is the best overview of where this idea came from.
